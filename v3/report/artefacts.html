Why BC_klein needs QX

The four sets in 38 where BC_klein failed and QX rescued it — the scoreboard V3 is measured against. Each case shows the reference chain that produced the failure, all four arms on the same person and seed, and the defect region magnified. The claim being shown: three of the four are not render artefacts at all, and every one of them is the crop's cut boundary reappearing in the output.

klein treats image 2 as a picture to imitate, not as a garment to parse. Whatever the subtractive crop leaves at its boundary is reproduced in the output — a flat neck cut becomes a missing collar, an alpha fringe becomes a hem that dissolves, and a crop with no garment silhouette left in it produces no edit at all. QX regenerates the garment onto white, so its reference has no cut boundary to copy. That is the entire reason the two arms have zero overlapping failures.

Three bands, named by what reached the output

Working vocabulary, extending V2's “attention deficit”. The bands are defined by what arrived in the output relative to the garment, which is observable, rather than by attention, which we have never looked at.

Over-attentionMore arrived than the garment. Content that is not the garment survives into the output: the reference's pose, its cut boundary, its matte fringe, its wearer's identity, its background.p012 collar · p007 hem · V2's identity import at −0.933 margin
Questionable attentionSome of the garment arrived, incompletely. The right region is attended and resolved wrong or half — a shallow collar where a stand collar belongs, a hue that shifted, a pattern that smoothed.most of the 6 BC and 17 QX ok verdicts · the whole drift table
Failed attentionNone of the garment arrived. The reference contributed no usable signal at the timesteps that decide layout, and the output is the input. HD_p023, both pairings
Over-attention has two sources, and they are the subtract/regenerate split. A subtractive arm gets more than the garment by copying what was beside it. A regenerative arm gets more than the garment by inventing what was not there — QX's speckle on a plain black tee. Same band, opposite mechanism, which is exactly why the two arms have no shared failures.
The open question this vocabulary makes askable. QX raises the floor by forcing the reference into a canonical view, and in doing so discards drape, rotation and pattern — ×0.51 of the edge detail on average. So: is the original context worth more than the interference it causes? The literature has the same tension on record — MV-VTON finds a single garment view “insufficient” and uses two; RefTon argues a worn reference reveals drape and translucency a flat shot cannot. Nobody has measured the trade for a reference like ours.
Read the SSIM column first, and then stop reading it. HD_p023 at 0.982 is klein handing back the photograph it was given — not a bad edit, no edit. That is the only thing this number separates: on the other three cases all four arms sit within 0.01 of each other, failure and rescue alike. A global SSIM finds no-ops and nothing else.
HD_p023 and HD_p023+p019 are the same garment reference against two different people — the 02–07 files in those two bundles are byte-identical — and both fail the same way. That is the evidence for the V2 finding that failure is a property of the garment, not the pairing, which is why it is worth fixing at the reference rather than at the output.

One measurement was tried here and did not survive, which is worth stating rather than quietly dropping. The intuition was that a subtractive crop is mostly bare skin where a regenerated reference is mostly garment, so a chroma skin test over the references should separate them. It does not. On p023 every reference scores 0.91–0.99 including QX's, because that garment is nude-coloured and a coarse YCrCb box cannot tell a beige tank from the arm wearing it. On p007 QX scores a clean 0% — but so would anything, since its non-white pixels are near-achromatic cream. Only p012, a dark navy, gives a number that means what it looks like. The metric is in the manifest and carries no signal; the argument on this page rests on the frames. That p023 defeats it is itself the case restating itself — the garment is the same value as the skin it is worn on, so after the head cut the crop is a low-contrast field with no silhouette in it.

the other sideQX's own failures · 20 perfect / 17 ok / 1 fail

Everything above is an argument for QX. This section is the argument against it, because V3 proposes to fold QX's mechanism into the single path, and it would inherit this too. QX has the highest floor of any arm and the lowest ceiling, and the two facts have the same cause: it does not subtract the context, it replaces the garment. What comes back is a garment that is cleaner than the crop and is not necessarily the same garment.

invented detailp017 + p002 · hair 1.9% · QX fail, both subtractive arms perfect

QX's only outright failure in 38 sets, and it is on a set neither subtractive arm has any trouble with. The garment is a plain black tee. QX's reference invents a whole ghost figure — tee, jeans and sneakers — and the output arrives covered in white speckle and scratch marks that exist nowhere in the input. This is the arm the V2 harness escalated to in order to route around AI artefacts, and it produced the only true speckle artefact in the evaluation.

What QX costs, and why this is not simply “use QX”

Shown in full above: QX's one outright failure and the drift table.

QX takes all four: perfect, perfect, perfect, ok. It is also 20 perfect / 17 ok / 1 fail over the same 38 sets — the lowest ceiling of any arm. Regeneration invents detail: hue drifts 21–30° on every reference (worst p009 at 88°), pattern retention falls to ×0.23 on p030, and only p019 and p028 come back clean. “No extraction arm currently returns the same garment.” The reviewer preferred the Qwen crops by eye while they were losing half the pattern — cleanest-looking and most faithful pull apart. V3's shape 1, klein as the extractor, inherits that trade, and klein's own extraction quality has never been measured; the numbers above are Qwen's.
