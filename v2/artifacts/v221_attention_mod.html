AC — Attention Modulation Test

Which way of controlling the garment reference does klein actually respond to?
Ten arms across four mechanisms, on the 20 pairs whose uncropped baseline failed. Raw inputs first, then every arm’s output — click ⓘ on any output to see the pipeline behind its reference. Click an image to zoom, ← → to step.
★ Attention Modulation/0 · AC-C destroy/1 · AC-A / AC-B/2 · Crops + PRE/3 · FASHN/4 · AC arms/5 · BG/6 · M
  • One question, four mechanisms. Every arm changes what klein attends to in the garment reference: remove the person (control, QX), remove only the head (BC), remove nothing but destroy identity (D1h/D2/D3 on both bases), or remove nothing at all (BALD). The run asks which mechanism klein responds to
  • Each row starts with the two raw inputs, untouched. Every output carries an ⓘ card showing the pipeline that produced its reference, ending with the exact image sent to klein — so a wrong result traces to the reference or to klein without leaving the row
  • /O arms cost nothing to produce — no generative step, deterministic, no licence question. /B arms need a bald frame first. If an /O arm matches its /B twin, the whole bald pipeline is unnecessary
  • BALD is the arm to watch. The bald photograph with no cropping at all. If it holds up, the cropper — BiRefNet, the parser, every variant in this phase — is not earning its place
  • Person image, prompt and seed (46) fixed; the only variable is the garment reference. Same 20 pairs whose uncropped baseline failed, so this compares directly against phase 2’s 75% / 45% split
  • Judge the garment, not the tidiness. §2b showed both instruments rewarding a plausible garment over the correct one — which is why this is an eye test
  • Ranking. Drag any output left or right — its position in the row is its rank, shown in the badge. Two bars split the row:
  • ▲ TOP TIES — everything before it is the top tier, all treated as equally good rather than forced into a false order. Those cells turn green
  • ▼ CUT OFF — everything after it does not count: greyed out, recorded as the discard pile
  • Both bars start out of the way — TOP TIES at the far left, CUT OFF at the far end — so every row opens as one plain ranked list with nothing discarded. Drag them inward as you decide. Everything between them is the ranked middle. Order saves to your browser as you go, so a refresh will not lose it. Save rankings CSV (bottom right) writes the file to send back — columns set_id, person, garment_source, arm, rank, tier, counted
  • Two conditions, and the split is the point. The original 20 pairs were chosen because the uncropped baseline failed — an attention criterion. The HIGH-DAMAGE rows were chosen by a different one: measured garment loss to the head cut. Only 5 of 11 damage references appeared in the original set, and the worst (p021, 19.5% lost) was never tested, so plain C3.1 was winning on pairs where its own known weakness was largely absent. These six close that hole. Judge them as their own group: does C3.1 fail here, and do the bald or pixelate arms rescue it?
  • Each damaged garment is now worn by three different people. With one person per garment a failure cannot be attributed — it could be the damage or the pairing. With three, the distinction is readable: fails on all three → the damage is the cause; fails on one → the pairing is. Rows carry a purple badge showing which replicate they are
  • QX covers 13 of 20 pairs; the fal balance ran out during reference prep. Weigh it as a smaller sample, not necessarily a weaker arm
