  • BG1 #FFFFFF — control, what ships today
  • BG2 flat #F1F1F1 — the shop-imagery model-shot spec; #FFFFFF is the packshot spec, and these crops are model shots
  • BG3 adaptive — a neutral ramp value chosen per reference to clear ≥15 ΔL* at the garment edge. Brown garment keeps white; white garment gets grey
  • BG4 BG3 + radial falloff + contact shadow — real packshot white is photographed, and flat synthetic #FFFFFF deletes exactly the separability cue the falloff provides
  • BG5 #FFFFFF + contact shadow, nothing else — isolates the shadow from the colour change. BG4 moves the ground and adds falloff and adds a shadow, so if BG4 wins we cannot say which part did the work. Here the ground stays white: packshot convention untouched, no way to tint the garment, and the cue is created locally at the silhouette — which works at any garment colour, white-on-white included
  • The question here is whether the detector called it right, not which image looks nicest. Each row prints the measured edge L*, the margin against white, and the resulting call.
  • The measure is an area share, not a median. A first version used the median luminance in a band inside the boundary and failed its own test case: p014, the one reference with a documented white-garment failure, is a white t-shirt over dark trousers — median L*=57 but 22% of the garment near-white, so a central statistic averaged the collision away. The table below ranks every reference by pale share so the 15% bar can be moved on evidence.
