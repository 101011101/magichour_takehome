Perfect-tier pick sheet — stop on perfect

Mark every cell perfect / ok / fail. The cascade stops at the first perfect arm, not the first tolerable one.
This replaces the binary usable/not sheet, which conflated ship it with acceptable and therefore optimised coverage instead of quality. It also replaces the old AMT tier as the label of record: AMT perfect meant tied for first among ten arms — a relative ranking — and an arm can top a weak field without being shippable, so a relative label cannot drive an absolute stop decision. Order is PHEAD (1 gen) → BC_klein (3) → QX (5). QX is last deliberately: it exists to route around AI artefacts, not to produce the best frame.
  • perfect = you would ship this to a user unchanged. ok = acceptable but you would rather have something better. fail = unusable. The distinction between the first two is the whole point of re-marking — the previous sheet could not see it.
  • The green halo is the arm that ships: the first perfect one. Cells past it dim to show they are never reached in production. Mark them anyway — they are the only evidence for whether the ordering is right.
  • Judge blind. Gate scores start hidden so your marks are not anchored to an instrument already measured at AUC 0.506 against you.
  • The header tracks generations per request, the share of sets shipping a perfect frame, how many have no perfect arm at all, and how often your new marks match the old AMT tier — that last number says whether the relative ranking was a safe stand-in.
  • Each row shows hair over garment, the candidate routing feature (BiRefNet matte minus the parser hair class, already on disk). It predicted PHEAD failure at AUC 0.918 on the binary marks; the CSV carries it so it can be re-tested against perfection rather than usability.
