base) without superseding it. Setup and test-set definitions:
[CONDITIONS.md](CONDITIONS.md). Full detail, per-set:

Last updated: **2026-08-19** · spend to date ≈ $19.6 fal + ~$3.3 judging.

## Finding

**A garment reference that contains a person causes an attention deficit, and
cropping the person out of it fixes most of the resulting failures.**

Measured by human review over 33 sets (13 Testset2 pairs + 20 person-to-person
combinations), 5 arms each: uncropped base plus four crop variants.

| | |
|---|---|
| Uncropped baseline failed | **20 of 33 sets (61%)** |
| Strongest crop arm | **C3.1 `no_face`** — solved **15 of 20 (75%)** |
| Equal on rate, conditional | **C4 `clothes_only`** — 15 of 20, but only where the cut is clean |
| Clearly weaker | C2 and C3.2 — **9 of 20 (45%)** each |
| Residual | **25% of baseline failures survive the best crop** — the target of phase 3 |

Baseline failure categories: wrong clothes 36%, wrong person 33%, wrong
background 33%, duplication 15%, no transfer 9%. Those three leading categories
occurring at near-identical rates is the signature of **one cause, not three** —
the model attending to the whole reference rather than the garment in it.

Supporting evidence from the earlier automated pass on the person-to-person set:
identity substitution fell from **25 of 83 (30%) uncropped to 3 of 83 (3.6%)
cropped**. Treat that as directional only — the same run also showed that a
no-op output scores perfectly on identity preservation, so the automated identity
metric cannot stand alone (see caveats).

## Why the residual 25% failed

The rate separates the arms — C3.1 and C4 at 75%, C2 and C3.2 at 45% — but it does
not say *why* the survivors survived. The failure descriptions do, and they
identify three mechanisms:

1. **Jagged cut edges are read as garment.** Where hair removal leaves a ragged
   boundary or a white notch, klein renders that white region as white cloth or
   as part of the design. The one set no arm solved failed exactly this way.
2. **A white background collides with a white garment.** A white t-shirt was
   ignored entirely because it matched the ground. White is right for a
   low-attention ground and wrong when the garment is also white.
3. **Body similarity helps, difference hurts.** Similar builds transfer easily;
   keeping the source wearer's body carries their attributes across, observed as
   a skin-colour mismatch in C2 and C3.

## Caveats

1. **Human review is the verdict, and it disagreed with the instruments.** Three
   of four baseline failures identified by eye on Testset2 were missed by the
   deterministic metrics and the VLM judge; one scored above average. See
   [../v2.2/EXPERIMENT.md](../v2.2/EXPERIMENT.md) section 2b for the traceability
   record.
2. **No-ops score as successes on identity metrics.** 22 of 165 outputs in the
   review set were near-identical to their input. Any identity-preservation
   number read without a no-op filter says the opposite of the truth. This
   invalidated a first-pass automated conclusion during this workstream.
3. **Sample size.** 33 sets, one reviewer, one seed. Directional.
4. **Parity rule outstanding.** All numbers are fal numbers.

## Checkpoint — 2026-08-19

**v2.2's question was "did the model make the correct change?", and the attention
deficit behind F1 and F2 is now addressed.** Three arms solve it by different
mechanisms and rescue each other:

| arm | mechanism | perfect / fail (38 sets) | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BC_klein** | bald → crop (subtract) | 74% / 5% | 1 extra call |
| **QX_qwen_p1** | Qwen extraction (regenerate) | 58% / 8% | 1 extra call |
| **PHEAD** | parser head-removal (subtract) | 63% / 21% | **free** |
| *baseline* `control` | C3.1 as it shipped | 53% / 34% | free |

**BC_klein + QX = 100% usable across all 38 sets**, with **five failures between them
and zero overlap** — every case where one falls over, the other is fine. The reason is
structural: subtraction cannot recover what it never saw, regeneration cannot
reproduce what it never captured, so the two have no shared failure mode.

### What v2.2.3 has to do

| half | runs | job |
|---|---|---|
| **Gate** | after generation | detect an unusable frame and **escalate to a different arm** |
| **Routing** | before generation | predict which arm this reference needs |

**The gate cannot work by reseeding.** Failure is a property of the **garment** — a
damaged reference failed on all three people it was paired with — so retrying the same
arm reproduces the failure. The gate must switch mechanism, not seed. That
invalidates the original v2.2.3 spec's retry design and is the single most important
correction carried into it.

Escalation ladder: **PHEAD (free) → BC_klein → QX**, expected ~1.3 calls against a
flat 2.0, with no floor case because the pair covers 100% usable.

### On V3 and cost

Routing is **a** cost lever, not the whole of V3. It is also **not yet validated** —
the router's features were selected after seeing which way they pointed, on 22
garments with 8 weak, so 75%-precision-at-8 is fitted and evaluated on the same data.
Two things are owed before it can be claimed: recompute over all 48 references, and
add FashionSigLIP centroid distance for garment unusualness.

The larger untested V3 lever remains **a cheaper base model given a clean
reference** — if attention is the bottleneck rather than capability, a smaller model
with a good reference may match a larger one with a poor reference, and that saving is
per-generation rather than per-preprocessing-step.

## Next

**One piece remains in v2.2: v2.2.3 — the failure gate plus routing.** It closes the
workstream.

- **Gate first, routing second.** A gate can recover from a bad route; a route cannot
  recover from a bad gate. The gate must **escalate mechanism, not reseed** — failure
  is a garment property, so a retry on the same arm reproduces it.
- The **no-op detector** stays a requirement, per caveat 2: an output identical to the
  input scores perfectly on identity metrics, so without it any identity number reads
  backwards.
- **Then v2.3** (artifacts) and **v2.4** (auxiliary cleanup).

**Superseded by the 2026-08-19 checkpoint**, kept so the change is visible rather than
silent:

- *v2.2.2 person crop and composite* — **closed as obsolete.** It was built to protect
  the background, but background damage proved to be a symptom of the attention
  deficit rather than a separate failure: 82% of wrong-background sets also had the
  wrong person, against 9% when the person was right. v2.2.1 removed the cause.
- *The three mechanism fixes* (hair-cut jaggedness, mannequin C5, non-white ground) —
  jaggedness was addressed inside the AC work; the mannequin and the ground were built,
  never klein-tested, and are now **dropped** to the parking lot with their trigger
  conditions unmet.

**Still owed regardless of what comes next: the self-hosted parity run.** Every number
in this document is a fal number, and V2's premise is open weights in the deploy path.
That gap is the one most likely to matter in review.
