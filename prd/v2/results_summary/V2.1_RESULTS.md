## Decision (Ray, 2026-08-14): klein 4B **distilled** is the editing base

**FLUX.2 klein 4B distilled (`fal-ai/flux-2/klein/4b/distilled/edit`) is the
model we are moving forward with.** The undistilled `base` sibling is a separate
checkpoint, measured below and not chosen. It is the strongest
arm on all three things the product is judged on — **fidelity, realism, and
garment transfer** — and it is Apache 2.0, ~13GB, sub-second, so it is
self-hostable without an A100. The FASHN-vs-klein contest recorded in V2.0 is
closed; FASHN v1.5 remains documented as the fallback for the
strict-preservation case (see below).

Evidence (Testset2, 13 high-res pairs, blind gpt-5.5 + deterministic):

| arm | VLM fidelity | VLM realism | garment | det score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **klein_4b_edit** | **4.41** | **4.03** | **4.08** | 0.811 |
| qwen_2511 (baseline) | 4.28 | 3.95 | 3.77 | 0.726 |
| fashn_v15 | 4.25 | 3.83 | 3.33 | 0.835 |
| hidream_o1_edit | 3.62 | 4.08 | 3.08 | 0.565 |

klein also wins on the V1 `test_set/` deterministic composite (0.778, top of
triage), so it leads on both sets.

### Known downsides of klein — accepted, and what mitigates each

These are real and must be designed around rather than ignored:

| Downside | Observed | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Occasional AI artifacts** | Distilled-model texture/detail artifacts in some outputs; realism 4.03 is good, not perfect | The auxiliary realism stage exists for exactly this — current leader `seedvr2_x2_noise0`. Note the aux screen's finding that a global pass does **not** repair *logical* artifacts (bad hands), so a region-targeted repair step may still be needed |
| **Occasional generation failure** | One solid-black frame in 16 triage runs (~6%) — a known distilled-model flake | Best-of-N retry with a new seed plus an automatic blank/low-variance output check; the retry machinery already exists in the composite harness (AuraFace gate + reseed) |
| **Occasional poor attention on edit, especially with multiple identities** | Weakest on `duo_swap` (fidelity 3.89 vs FASHN's 4.22) — the kind where the garment reference is itself a person, i.e. two identities in the prompt | Explicit target-garment designation in the prompt ("take *the grey suit* worn by the person in image 2; do not copy their face or background") — already in the harness and the reason `wrong_person` was 0.00 for klein across all 13 pairs. Keep `wrong_person` as a permanent regression check |

### Why not FASHN v1.5

FASHN wins identity (AuraFace 0.971 vs 0.903), background (perfect 5.00 scene,
27.5 dB) and the deterministic composite (0.835) — it preserves the original
photo better than anything tested. But it transfers garments **conservatively**
(VLM garment 3.33, the lowest of the three serious arms), and garment transfer
is the core product objective (×2 weight). It also carries a hard availability
risk: it runs pose detection in-pipeline and **errors out entirely** when that
fails (`Failed to detect body pose in model image`, 1 of 13 pairs), where klein
has no such precondition.

**FASHN stays documented as the pick when strict original-photo preservation is
the requirement** — the same role it held in V1 — and as the fallback if klein's
failure rate proves worse at volume.

## klein distilled vs klein base (2026-08-15) — same family, different trade

The V2.0 run tested only the **distilled** klein. fal also serves the
**undistilled base** (`fal-ai/flux-2/klein/4b/base/edit`, Apache 2.0, $0.009/MP,
true CFG + negative prompt). Same 13 Testset2 pairs, same seed and prompt, plus a
negative prompt naming the failure modes ("different person, changed face,
changed background, extra limbs, deformed hands").

| arm | fidelity | realism | garment | identity | scene | id cos | bg psnr | det |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| klein_4b_edit (distilled) | **4.41** | 4.03 | **4.08** | 4.46 | 4.69 | 0.903 | 22.9 | **0.811** |
| klein_4b_base_edit | **4.41** | **4.08** | 3.54 | **4.69** | **5.00** | **0.922** | **25.1** | 0.796 |

1. **Dead heat on fidelity, opposite composition.** Identical 4.41, but the base
   gets there through preservation (identity 4.69, perfect 5.00 scene, +2.2 dB
   background) where the distilled gets there through transfer (garment 4.08 vs
   3.54). The base sits between distilled klein and FASHN on the same axis the
   V2.0 contest turned on.
2. **The base wins the hardest kind.** On `duo_swap` it scores fidelity 4.222
   (distilled 3.889) *and* the best realism of any arm on any kind (4.333) —
   ties FASHN's lead on the kind FASHN led.
3. **No resolution confound** (corrected 2026-08-15). An earlier note here
   claimed the base rendered smaller frames. It does not: output dimensions are
   **identical on all 13 pairs** (both 1.01–1.04 MP; e.g. ts2_02 is 832×1248 for
   both). The metrics are a fair comparison, and the visible quality difference
   is model behaviour, not resolution. (The arm that does render small is FASHN,
   0.39–0.89 MP — the likely source of the original figure.)
4. **No distillation flake in this run.** Zero failures in 13; `wrong_person`
   0.00. Caveat: the distilled failure it is compared against is **one pair at
   one seed** (ts2_12), so "flake" versus "unlucky seed" is not yet separable —
   a reseed at ~4 steps costs almost nothing and would likely erase the gap.
5. **Confounded by the negative prompt.** The base ran *with* a preservation-only
   negative prompt; the distilled cannot accept one. Every term in it pushes the
   model to change less, which plausibly produces both the base's high
   identity/scene **and** its weak garment (3.54) — the conservative profile
   FASHN has. An isolation run (base, same seeds, no negative prompt) is required
   before attributing the difference to the checkpoint.
6. **Human review agrees with the garment gap.** Ray reviewed the outputs
   side by side (`v2/artifacts/klein_compare.html`) and judged the base clearly
   worse by eye. Human review is the final tiebreaker in SCORING_CRITERIA, and it
   points the same way as the ×2-weighted garment score.
7. **Does not overturn the V2.1 decision** — garment transfer is the ×2 objective
   and distilled still leads it, human review agrees, and the base costs roughly
   an order of magnitude more compute (28 steps at guidance 5, i.e. true CFG =
   two forward passes per step, versus ~4 steps at CFG 0). Distilled can run
   best-of-3 with reseeds for a fraction of one base generation — which addresses
   the only thing the base won on. Revisit only if the isolation run shows the
   negative prompt was suppressing its garment transfer, or for LoRA fine-tuning,
   where base is the checkpoint BFL recommends.

## Auxiliary two-batch run (2026-08-14) — SeedVR2 confirmed, with a control

40 outputs: 4 configs × 5 subjects × 2 batches. **Batch A = real Testset2 photos**
(a realism pass should be a no-op — whatever it changes is damage inflicted
regardless of input quality); **Batch B = klein outputs for the same five
subjects** (the deployed case). Page: `v2/artifacts/aux_batches.html`.

| batch | config | realism | fidelity | id cos | ssim | hf ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| klein | **seedvr2_x2_noise0** | **4.15** | 4.90 | **0.943** | 0.920 | 1.68 |
| klein | seedvr2_x2_noise01 | 4.10 | 5.00 | 0.892 | 0.913 | 1.62 |
| klein | seedvr2_then_zimage | 4.05 | 4.90 | 0.702 | 0.857 | 1.27 |
| klein | zimage_s025 | 4.00 | 4.80 | 0.613 | 0.853 | 0.84 |
| original | **seedvr2_x2_noise0** | **4.05** | 5.00 | **0.937** | 0.940 | 1.20 |
| original | seedvr2_x2_noise01 | 3.95 | 5.00 | 0.896 | 0.935 | 1.09 |
| original | seedvr2_then_zimage | 3.95 | 4.80 | 0.742 | 0.903 | 0.78 |
| original | zimage_s025 | 3.90 | 4.90 | 0.721 | 0.903 | 0.84 |

1. **`seedvr2_x2_noise0` wins both batches** on realism *and* identity
   preservation — it is the leader whether the input is a real photo or a
   generated one. Confirms the first screen's pick on independent data.
2. **The control exposes Z-Image as destructive.** On a *real photograph that
   needed no repair*, Z-Image still drops AuraFace to 0.72 (0.61 on klein
   outputs) and visibly restructures the face. That loss is pure damage, not a
   repair trade-off — the first screen could not see this because every BEFORE
   there was already AI-generated. Z-Image Turbo is now effectively out of the
   running; the Base + tile-CN variant remains the queued test.
3. **The `hf_ratio` confound from V2.0 is resolved.** SeedVR2 measures 1.20 on
   real photos versus 1.68 on klein outputs — the same model, the same settings.
   It adds high-frequency detail in proportion to how soft the input is, which is
   restoration behaviour, not gratuitous sharpening. Treat hf_ratio > ~1.5 as a
   signal that the input was soft, not that the model over-sharpened.
4. **SeedVR2 preserves identity better at higher resolution** — 0.94 here versus
   0.839 in the first screen, whose BEFOREs were 864×1296 FASHN outputs. Feeding
   it high-res inputs is worth doing.
5. **`artifact_fix` = 3.00 again, all 8 rows.** Across two independent rounds and
   14 config-batches, no global realism pass has ever repaired an artifact. This
   is now a settled finding, not a preliminary one: **artifact repair needs a
   region-targeted step, and it will not come from the auxiliary bucket.**

## What this changes

1. The composite target is now **klein → SeedVR2**, not FASHN → SeedVR2.
2. The V2.0 "tie-break the editing base" next-step is **closed** — no need to run
   both bases through the aux stage to decide; klein is decided. (Running FASHN
   through it remains optional as a fallback datapoint.)
3. The reliability work is now on the critical path: blank-frame detection +
   reseed, and keeping `wrong_person` as a standing regression check for the
   multi-identity attention weakness.

## Open caveats (carried forward)

1. **Sample size** — 13 pairs on Testset2, 4 on triage, 4 images on the aux
   screen. The decision is directional evidence plus product judgement, not a
   statistically settled result.
2. **Parity rule outstanding** — every number is from fal-hosted endpoints;
   klein must be re-run on downloaded weights (~13GB, T4-feasible at fp8) before
   any figure is claimed as final.
3. **Cross-kind comparison invalid** — duo garment scores are inflated relative
   to product (CONDITIONS.md §3).
4. **Logical artifacts unaddressed** — no auxiliary model repairs bad hands;
   klein's hands score (4.15 on triage) is better than FASHN's but a
   region-targeted repair step is still an open design question.
