# V2.x — potential directions

Parking lot for work that is real but **not scheduled**. Each entry records what
it is, the slot it would most likely occupy, and — the important column — the
**condition that would trigger it**. Nothing here is committed; a direction
leaves this document by being adopted into a workstream with its own
EXPERIMENT / PLAN / TEST / RESULTS.

Scheduled work lives in [v2.1](v2.1/) (realism, parked), [v2.2](v2.2/)
(attention + failure), [v2.3](v2.3/) (artifacts), [v2.4](v2.4/) (auxiliary,
deferred). Program state: [results_summary/](results_summary/).

## Direction 1 in detail — where region restore belongs

**What it is.** After generation, detect which regions changed, decide per region
whether the change was intended (the garment) or accidental (face, hands,
background), and composite the accidental ones back from the original. Designed
in [ideas/INTENTION_AWARE_FIDELITY.md](ideas/INTENTION_AWARE_FIDELITY.md); it was
originally drafted into v2.2 as component [C] and has been **removed from v2.2
scope** (2026-08-15) — v2.2 is attention and failure only.

**Why it is out of v2.2.** v2.2.2 attacks the same damage by a simpler route:
crop the person out, run the edit on the crop, composite back onto the original
background. Background preservation then holds **by construction** — the model
never sees the background, so it cannot repaint it. Region restore is the
after-the-fact version of the same goal: it needs registration, diffing, region
scoring and confidence thresholds, and it carries a laundering risk (a stage that
restores everything scores beautifully on preservation and deletes the product).
Doing the structural fix first is cheaper and may make the detective work
unnecessary.

**Two candidate slots, with the condition for each:**

| Slot | When | Condition to run |
|---|---|---|
| **v2.2.4** — immediately after v2.2.2 | The narrow version: restore only what the recrop failed to protect | Run **only if** v2.2.2's crop-and-composite leaves measurable damage — i.e. background or identity is still degraded inside the recropped region, or the composite seam is visible. If the recrop holds background and identity, this slot is skipped entirely |
| **v2.5** — after the composites exist | The general version: a preservation stage over the whole pipeline output, not just the editing step | Run if damage is still present once the full composite (edit → auxiliary realism → artifact repair) is assembled, since later stages can reintroduce drift the recrop prevented at the editing step |

**Decision rule:** measure after v2.2.2 first. If the recrop solves it, region
restore never gets built — that is the preferred outcome, and it is why the
measurement matters more than the design. If it is only partly solved, take the
narrow v2.2.4 slot scoped to the residual. If it is solved at the editing step
but reappears downstream, it belongs in v2.5 against the assembled pipeline.

**Do not** build it as a general guardrail before either measurement exists. The
design is finished and waiting; nothing is lost by leaving it parked.

## Direction 11 in detail — garment regeneration after over-cropping

**What triggered it.** The v2.2.1 sweep over 48 references (2026-08-15) showed the
head-removal step cutting into the collar on several worn references (p004, p022
are the clearest), and small-subject frames losing garment area entirely (p016,
mask 1.4%). The crop removes pixels that belong to the garment.

### The distinction that decides the whole approach

| Case | What is missing | What is needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Continue existing fabric** | A 5-20px collar band, a nicked sleeve edge, a hem notch — the garment's own texture continues into the hole | Deterministic fill is sufficient. No model needs to *invent* anything |
| **Invent missing structure** | A whole sleeve, a shoulder strap, the top third of a dress | A generative model. Nothing deterministic can produce structure that was never observed |

Nearly every failure observed so far is the **first** case. Treat the second as out
of scope until a real instance appears, because the model class needed for it is
an order of magnitude heavier.

### Ladder — cheapest first, stop as soon as it is good enough

| # | Approach | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Do not over-cut.** Erode the head mask before subtracting, or protect a collar band below the chin landmark | **Zero** — a threshold change | This is a masking bug before it is a regeneration problem. Try it first; it may close the whole direction |
| 1 | **Bias the crop conservatively** — leave a little neck and shoulder in the reference | Zero | The model needs to *understand* the garment, not receive a surgical cutout. Slightly under-cropping is strictly safer than over-cropping |
| 2 | **Mirror fill** — garments are largely bilaterally symmetric; a missing left collar can be copied from the right | Zero, numpy | Works on symmetric garments, fails on asymmetric cuts and directional prints. Cheap to gate on a symmetry check |
| 3 | **`cv2.inpaint`** (Telea / Navier-Stokes) | Zero, milliseconds | Fine on smooth fabric, smears on patterns and text. Adequate for a 10px collar band |
| 4 | **Texture synthesis from the garment's own pixels** — sample the garment interior, tile into the hole, match at the boundary | Zero, numpy | Better than Telea on patterned fabric; a frequency-separation trick rather than a model |
| 5 | **MI-GAN** (MIT, mobile-class inpainter) | Smallest real model | Purpose-built for on-device inpainting; the lightest generative option worth testing |
| 6 | **Moebius** (Apache 2.0, **0.22B**, ECCV 2026) | Very light | Claims FLUX-Fill parity at >15x speed. Mask-fill only, not text-guided — which is exactly this use case. Catalog section 4.4 |
| 7 | **LaMa** (Apache, CPU-capable via IOPaint) | Light, CPU-viable | The established workhorse for hole filling; better structure continuation than Telea |
| 8 | **Z-Image-Turbo inpaint** (Apache, 6B) | Heaviest here | Already in the stack, so no new dependency — but far more model than a collar band needs |

**Preferred order to actually try:** 0 and 1 first (they may eliminate the need),
then 3 or 4 for residual gaps, then Moebius or MI-GAN only if deterministic fill
visibly damages patterned fabric. Anything at tier 8 for a 10px band is
over-engineering.

### Cost discipline

This stage would run **per garment reference**, and garment references are
cacheable per catalog image, so even a model here amortises well. That is an
argument for correctness over raw speed — but it is not an argument for reaching
past tier 6, because a 0.22B mask-filler and a 6B diffusion model produce
indistinguishable results on a collar band.

### Trigger condition

Run **only if** the over-cut is shown to degrade the model's output — i.e. the
v2.2.1 klein trials show C3/C4 losing to the uncropped baseline on references
with visible over-cutting, or human review flags collars and sleeves as wrong in
the generated results. If klein produces correct garments despite a slightly
nicked reference, this direction is unnecessary and should stay parked: the
reference only has to convey the garment, not be pixel-perfect.

**Do not build this before the klein trials.** The over-cut is currently a defect
in an intermediate artifact, and it is not yet known whether it matters
downstream.

**Fired — 2026-08-15.** The klein trials answered it, and worse than the trigger
anticipated. The failure is not that a nicked collar produces a slightly wrong
collar; it is that **klein reads the jagged white cut edge as white cloth or as
part of the garment's design** and renders it. On
`man_black_suit + woman_top_denim_skirt` — the one set of 33 that *no* crop arm
solved — "the hair jaggedness has caused the clothing to be incorrect."

Two consequences for the ladder above:

- **Tier 0 is now the priority, not a formality.** The defect is edge *quality*,
  not missing area, so eroding and feathering the head mask attacks the actual
  cause. A smooth boundary may close this direction without any fill at all.
- **Regeneration alone would not fix it.** Filling a hole with plausible fabric
  still leaves the boundary the model is misreading. Edge treatment comes first;
  fill is for what remains after it.

The mechanism is shared with direction 13: in both cases a white region adjacent
to the garment gets promoted to garment. Fixing either in isolation leaves the
other.

## Direction 12 in detail — a mannequin body instead of a person or a hole

**The gap it fills.** The current crop ladder has two failure shapes at its top end:

| variant | what it does | how it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **C3.2** | keeps the wearer, removes the face | the body is still a *specific person* — skin tone, build, tattoos, hands. Identity information the model can latch onto |
| **C4** | removes all skin | leaves **holes**. Observed: hands crossed over a jacket punch a gap through the garment front; sleeves end in nothing |

Both are wrong in opposite directions. A garment needs *a body* to hang on — drape,
fit and silhouette are only legible when something fills the clothes — but it does
not need *that person's* body.

**The idea.** Replace the wearer's visible skin with a **neutral grey form**: a
body-shaped fill with no face, no skin tone, no identifying features. The garment
keeps its shape and drape; the human disappears.

This is the **ghost-mannequin** convention that e-commerce photography already
uses, which is the strongest argument for it: it is **in-distribution** for models
trained on shop imagery, in a way that both a headless human and a floating
garment with holes are not.

### Architecture

The masks already exist — `garment_crop.py` computes body-skin, face-skin and hair
classes from Selfie Multiclass and the subject alpha from BiRefNet. C4 subtracts
them; this direction **fills them instead**. That is the whole change in principle.

| # | Approach | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Flat grey fill** — set the skin/face/hair region to a constant neutral grey inside the subject matte | **Zero** — a one-line change from C4 (fill instead of subtract) | Body reads as a flat silhouette; may look like a grey garment rather than a form |
| 1 | **Luminance-shaded grey** — modulate the grey by the original region's normalised luminance | Zero, numpy | Keeps the shading that makes a body read as 3D, discards colour and texture. The obvious first real attempt |
| 2 | **Depth- or normal-shaded grey** — shade using Depth Anything V2 Small or Sapiens2 normals rather than the source luminance | One small model | Cleaner form, immune to the original lighting; worth it only if (1) looks flat |
| 3 | **Generative ghost-mannequin** — inpaint the skin region as mannequin material with a light inpainter (Moebius, MI-GAN) | Light model | Can also close the C4 holes in the same pass. Shares machinery with direction 11 |
| 4 | **Posed 3D mannequin** — fit SMPL-X to pose landmarks and render a grey body | Heavy | Highest quality, but a mesh-fitting pipeline plus SMPL-X licence review. Only if the flat approaches fail |

**Start at tier 1.** It is free, reuses masks we already compute, and answers the
question. Tiers 3 and 4 are only worth reaching for if a shaded grey body is
demonstrably confusing the model.

### Design details that will decide whether it works

- **Choose a grey that cannot read as fabric.** A flat matte mid-grey with no
  texture and no colour cast. If the model interprets the body as a grey garment,
  the whole idea inverts into a new failure mode — this is the main risk and the
  first thing to check in outputs.
- **Keep the boundary honest.** The garment/skin boundary comes from the 256x256
  class map, so it is soft. Fill slightly *inside* the garment edge rather than
  outside, so a mask error leaves a sliver of grey under the garment rather than
  eating the hem.
- **Hands over garments are the test case.** The C4 hole on `man_black_suit` is
  the specific defect this should fix — a grey hand in front of a jacket is
  legible; a hole through the jacket is not.
- **Hair is a separate decision.** Grey-filling hair is likely wrong; hair over
  shoulders is neither garment nor mannequin. Probably keep the C3.2 treatment
  (leave hair) and grey only skin. Worth testing both.

### Relationship to the rest of the ladder

This becomes **C5**, sitting above C4: C1 bbox, C2 background removed, C3.1 no
head, C3.2 no face, C4 clothes only, **C5 clothes on a grey mannequin**. It goes
through the same klein trials as the others, at the same cost (~$0.015 per pair).

### A prompted variant arrives from AC (2026-08-18)

The **ghost-mannequin prompt** was tried in the AC extraction trials — *"show this
outfit as a ghost mannequin product shot on white: the clothing keeps its shape and
drape as if worn, but the person is invisible"* — and failed **as an extraction
method**: mean lightness drift of 48 on Qwen 2511 and 99 on Plus, because the model
reads it as *make it pale and simple* rather than *keep the drape*.

It is moved here rather than discarded, because that result is about extraction and
not about the idea. Asking a model to supply a neutral body **is** a mannequin
experiment, and it belongs beside M1 and M2 as a third way of producing one:

| | how the mannequin is produced |
|---|---|
| M1 | flat grey fill, deterministic |
| M2 | shaded grey, deterministic |
| **M3-p** | **prompted** — the model supplies the form |

Worth testing here for the obvious reason: if a model can supply a convincing
mannequin, the deterministic one may never need building. Worth doubting for an
equally obvious one — the measured drift says that when asked for a ghost mannequin
these models simplify the garment, which is the opposite of what M exists to protect.

### Trigger condition

Run **if the v2.2.1 review shows the ladder failing at both ends** — C4 losing to
C3.2 because of holes, and C3.2 still leaking identity or attention. The
annotation CSV from `v2/artifacts/v221_review.html` answers this directly: if `solved_C4` is
frequently "not solved" on sets whose base failure was *wrong person*, while
`solved_C3_2` is also weak, a mannequin is the missing middle.

Do not build it if C3.2 is already solving the identity failures — the simplest
variant that works should ship, and adding a synthetic body is strictly more
machinery than removing a face.

**Fired — 2026-08-15**, and by the exact test written above. The trigger asked
whether C3.2 is already solving the identity failures: it is not. **C3.2 solves 9
of 20 (45%)** against C3.1 and C4 at 75%, so the "simplest variant that works"
escape clause does not apply. The ladder fails at both ends, and the failure
descriptions say how:

- **C4's holes are worse than predicted.** They are not merely missing area — the
  white gap is rendered as white cloth (direction 11). C4's solve rate is
  conditional on a clean cut, which is precisely what a mannequin fill provides.
- **A kept body leaks more than identity.** `p018 + p016` recorded a **skin-colour
  mismatch in C2 and C3** — the source wearer's body attributes crossing into the
  result. That is the concrete instance of "the body is still a specific person".

So the mannequin now has both halves of its argument measured: it removes what C2
and C3 leak, and it fills what C4 punches out. It remains the more expensive
option, so it should be built **after** tier 0 of direction 11 — if feathering the
head mask makes C4's edges clean, C4 may be sufficient and C5 unnecessary.

## Direction 13 in detail — ground colour chosen per reference

**What triggered it.** On `p018 + p014` in the v2.2.1 review, a white t-shirt was
not transferred at all. The reviewer's note names the cause: the garment "seems to
be the same colour as the background", and proposes the fix — "perhaps we have a
checkerboard in the background to differentiate."

### Why white was chosen, and why it is still mostly right

The cropper flattens to white deliberately. White is the lowest-information ground
available: it carries no texture the model can read as fabric, it matches the
e-commerce packshot convention the model was trained on, and it is what a default
alpha flatten should be (a black flatten would be far worse). None of that changes.

The defect is narrow and conditional: **when the garment is itself white or
near-white, a white ground stops being neutral and starts being camouflage.** The
garment/ground boundary is where the model finds the garment, and a white shirt on
white has no boundary. This is the same mechanism as direction 11 seen from the
other side — there, white ground was promoted *to* garment; here, white garment
was demoted *to* ground.

### The fix, cheapest first

| # | Approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Trigger on the garment, not always.** Compute the garment region's mean luminance and its contrast against the ground; only swap when the margin is below a threshold | The whole direction is conditional. A garment that already separates should keep white — do not regress the common case to fix the rare one |
| 1 | **Flat mid-grey ground** | The simplest separable ground, still texture-free. Try before a checker: if a solid tone restores the boundary, no pattern is needed |
| 2 | **Low-contrast checkerboard** | The reviewer's suggestion. Keeps the ground low-information while guaranteeing a boundary at any garment colour. Risk: a periodic pattern is exactly the kind of structure a model can read as fabric weave or print — keep the cells large and the contrast small |
| 3 | **Complementary ground picked from the garment's dominant colour** | Most robust, most machinery. Only if a fixed alternate ground fails on some garment colour |

Tier 0 is mandatory regardless of which of 1–3 is chosen; the others are a ladder.

### How to test it

Cheap and self-contained: the trigger condition selects its own test set. Filter
the reference set for near-white garments, re-run only those through klein with
the alternate ground, and compare against their existing white-ground outputs.
Everything else is held fixed, so any difference is attributable to the ground. On
the order of a dozen pairs, well under a dollar.

**Watch for the regression this could cause.** A patterned or coloured ground is
more information than white, and the entire premise of v2.2.1 is that extra
information in the reference steals attention from the garment. Include a handful
of *non*-white garments in the test to confirm the alternate ground does not hurt
the cases white already handles — that is the result that would keep this
conditional rather than making it the default.

### Relationship to the other two

Directions 11, 12 and 13 are one problem with three faces: **the boundary between
garment and not-garment is what the model actually reads.** Direction 11 fixes a
ragged boundary, direction 12 replaces the thing on the far side of it, and
direction 13 makes it visible when the colours collide. Sequence them tier-0-first
across all three before building anything heavier in any one of them.
