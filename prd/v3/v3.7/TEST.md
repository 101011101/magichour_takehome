# v3.7 — TEST

The matrix, per [SCHEMA.md](../SCHEMA.md). What is combined with what, and why that and
not more.

---

## 1. The fold

`v3/testsets/v35_linkC.csv` — **51 pairs**, 33 unique garments, generated by
`v3/build/make_v35_linkC_set.py` and rendered here rather than typed, per
[SCHEMA](../SCHEMA.md#v3xtestmd--the-matrix). This is the union of the
v3.3 failures and the v3.4 lock's own, the set v3.5 link C runs on, chosen for one reason:
`BC`'s references and `BC`'s cells at seed 46 **already exist** for every pair of it, in
`v35_linkC_20260910_0513.zip`. The incumbent's side of the comparison is therefore not
re-generated, not re-judged and not re-paid for; it is the record.

Hard pairs rather than a random sample is the right bias here. The arm's whole claim is
about pose, and this fold is where the lock's written pose clause already failed.

## 2. The arms

| arm | call 1 | call 2 | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BC` | bald the raw garment photo, one image | `v3lib.EDIT_PROMPT` | reused, not recomputed |
| `BCp` | **+ the target photo as image 2**, re-pose asked third | identical | new |
| `BCp2` | same two images, image 2 declared a pose reference, pose asked first | identical | new |

Everything downstream of call 1 is held: the same V2 head-subtracting crop
(`phase3_variants.masks(cranium=True)`, the exact call that makes every `BC` reference),
the same call-2 prompt, the same seed 46 as the iron-man 2 and link C records. **One input
added to one call is the only difference between `BCp` and `BC`.**

### Why two wordings and not one

A single failing sentence proves nothing about a model. `BCp` asks in the shape the arm was
specified in — bald first, re-pose third. `BCp2` states image 2's role before asking for
anything, asks for the pose first, and then forbids taking anything else from it. If klein
can read a pose out of a second image at all, one of the two should show it. If neither
does, the capability is absent rather than badly asked for — which is the distinction the
verdict turns on.

### Why no third variant

Not run, deliberately. Two wordings that differ in order, role declaration and prohibition
already span the reasonable space; a third would be prompt-tuning an arm whose failure mode
is not prompt-shaped. The 18 collapses are not a wording problem — the model is answering a
different question than the one asked, and it answers it the same way under both.

## 3. Seeds

**One seed, 46.** Seed stability is not the question — the question is whether a capability
exists, and a capability that only appears at one seed in three is not one the deploy path
can use. Seed 46 is the iron-man 2 and link C record's first seed, so every new cell is
paired with a `BC` cell a human has already judged.

## 4. What gets measured, and on how much

| stage | pairs | why |
|---|---|---|
| call 1, both wordings | **51** — all of them | the arm's entire change lives here |
| V2 crop + call 2 | **a subset** | the V2 cropper is ~1½ min of laptop CPU per reference; the verdict does not turn on it |

The end-to-end cells are a sanity check that the reference behaves as its call-1 frame
predicts, not the evidence. If call 1 hands back the source pose, the reference is `BC`'s
reference and call 2 has nothing new to do; if call 1 hands back the target re-dressed, the
reference is already wrong and no call 2 repairs it.

## 5. Budget

Call 1 is 2 calls per pair (one per wording) and call 2 is 1 per pair per arm on whatever
subset is cropped: **≤ 4 calls/pair**, ~$0.06, so the whole fold is under $4 at
$0.015/call. Actual spend and wall time → [RESULTS §1](RESULTS.md#1-the-run-2026-09-10).

**The per-pair call cost is itself a variable under test, not an aside.** `BC` amortises one
bald call across every pair sharing a garment — on this fold 33 garments over 51 pairs, so
0.65 + 1 edit = **1.65 calls/pair**. `BCp`'s reference depends on the target and cannot be
shared: **2.00 calls/pair**, **+21%**. An arm that costs a fifth more has to buy something,
and that is the bar it is held to.

## 6. Review protocol

**Instrument first, human second, and the verdict may rest on the instrument alone.**
Pose is read with the pipeline's own detector, not by eye — `v3/build/v37_pose_metrics.py`,
per-pair output in the run's `meta/pose_metrics.json`. Three numbers per pair: which input
the output resembles globally, which pose it is nearer, and what fraction of pixels moved.
Method and thresholds → [RESULTS §3](RESULTS.md#3-method--and-why-shift-alone-would-have-misled).

`v3/report/v37.html` carries the vote widget for a human pass over the same cells
(pose: moved / partial / ignored; leak: clothing / face-body / background / no), exporting
`v37_votes.csv`. A human pass is required to *accept* the arm. It is not required to reject
one whose reference frames are wrong by measurement, and none was run.
